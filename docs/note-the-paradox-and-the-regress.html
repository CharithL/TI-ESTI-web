The Paradox and the Regress

Six formulations of the paradox of analysis, set side by side — and the objection reformulated so that it stands in the same family and can be argued with.

3 September 2026 · all six quoted from the primary texts
The result, first

The six formulations are not variants of one thing. They differ in what sameness is at issue and in what the first horn accuses the analysis of. Husserl’s first horn is circularity; Langford’s is triviality. That difference decides everything, because the two horns require opposite escapes, and each escape aggravates the other.

The objection is then not a rival to the paradox. It is what you get when you take the standard escape from the circularity horn and apply (PD) to the escape. Stated that way it becomes a dilemma of the same shape as the others, with a first horn that does real work — and it catches Clark, who walks into that horn explicitly.

§1  The six, side by side

Each is given in the same schema: the pair being related, the relatum in which sameness is asserted, the two horns, and the escape the author takes or refuses.

1
Husserl — the original
1891

pair: definiendum / definiens  ·  relatum: sense — what I “think with” the expression

Horn 1 — samenessIf with the word being defined I already clearly think everything I think with the defining expression, we have the “obvious circle”.
Horn 2 — differenceIf the defining expression has a more richly articulated sense, I do not think the same with it, and so the definition is incorrect.
Escape takenNone. Husserl concludes the paradox is irresoluble and that analysis is impossible.
2
Frege — the dissolution
1894

pair: two definitions of a term  ·  relatum: changed — extension, not sense

Horn 1 — droppedCircularity is not a defect if sense-preservation was never the requirement.
Horn 2 — droppedTwo definitions with different senses are “neither more correct nor more incorrect”; the choice is “based on convenience.”
Escape takenDrop to extension: “a coincidence of extension is a sufficient and necessary criterion.” Cost: Dummett shows the criterion is “patently inadequate” — the two definitions of a conic are provably coextensive, and mere material equivalence would satisfy no mathematician. It also silently abandons the conceptual-priority requirement Frege insists on in Grundlagen §64.
3
Langford — the naming
1942

pair: analysandum / analysans  ·  relatum: meaning of the verbal expressions

Horn 1 — samenessSame meaning → the analysis “states a bare identity and is trivial.”
Horn 2 — differenceDifferent meaning → the analysis is incorrect.
Escape takenNone offered in 1942. In 1964 Langford splits analysis into formal (synonymous, less idiomatic, verbal component shows the complexity of the conceptual one) and synthetic (not synonymous, necessarily codenoting) — which relocates the problem rather than dissolving it.
4
Moore — the confession
1942

pair: “to be a brother is to be a male sibling” / “to be a brother is to be a brother”  ·  relatum: concept

Horn 1Same concept → the two statements should be the same statement. They are not.
Horn 2Different concept → the analysis is false.
Escape attemptedBoth statements are “in some sense about the expressions used as well as about the concept.” Then: “in what sense they are about the expressions used I cannot see clearly; and therefore I cannot give any clear solution to the puzzle.” An admitted terminus — and note the shape of the move: a retreat from the object to the question’s own vehicle.
5
Lewy — the premises made explicit
1976

pair: (A) concept of vixen = concept of female fox  /  (B) concept of vixen = concept of vixen

Premise (1)(A) entails nothing about the word ‘vixen’ or the expression ‘female fox’.
Premise (2)If (A) and (1), then the proposition (A) is the proposition (B) — which is paradoxical.
Why this version mattersIt exposes the hidden premise: propositions are individuated by the concepts they contain. Every solution is now locatable as a denial. Deny (1) → the metalinguistic family (Sellars, White, Rieber). Deny (2) → Moore’s route, per Lewy. Deny (A) → no concept-identity claim is ever true, which nobody will accept.
6
Clark — the paradox as a requirement
2022

pair: ‘F’ / its ousia  ·  relatum: primary intension; “sameness in cognitive significance”

Horn 1 — not seen as a hornSocrates wants “an intensionally equivalent expression, or an informative synonym.”
Horn 2 — not seen as a hornSameness of meaning “appears to involve sameness in cognitive significance.”
Escape taken, unwittinglyForster’s articulation criterion: an informative synonym is “a synonym articulated by means of words, each of which signifies a concept other than that being defined.” Fear = the expectation of future evil is informative; dread is not. Plus asymmetry (n. 31): “If A defines B, it will not be the case that B defines A.”

The finding. The column that splits the table is the third. Two authors — the first and the last, 131 years apart — take the first horn to be circularity. The four in between take it to be triviality. These are different accusations and they call for opposite remedies.

To beat circularity you require that the definiens be articulated into concepts other than the definiendum. That is Forster’s criterion, Clark’s criterion, and Husserl’s own “more richly articulated sense.”

To beat triviality you require a difference in cognitive significance between the two sides. That is what makes the analysis informative.

But the remedy for circularity is the disease for correctness. Husserl saw this and stated it as his own second horn: the more richly articulated the definiens, the less I think the same with it, and so the more incorrect the definition. The articulation that saves you from the circle is the very thing that opens the gap in sense. Clark helps himself to the first half of Husserl’s sentence and does not read the second.

§3  The objection, reformulated

The objection was three things, stated informally. Each now has a place in the family above, and each can be given the same two-horned shape.

Objection I — the regress

As it stood: “what is F-ness? → z. and then what is z-ness? → y. and then what is y-ness… what is n-ness? this is an infinite regression.”

Reformulated: (PD) plus non-circularity generates the regress

The regress was never a consequence of (PD) alone. It is a consequence of (PD) together with the condition imposed to escape Husserl’s first horn — and that condition is not a private addition, it is what Forster and Clark themselves require of a Socratic answer.

(PD)
If A fails to know what F-ness is, then A fails to know anything about F-ness.
(NC)
An adequate answer to “What is F-ness?” must be articulated by means of concepts other than F-ness. — Forster 2006: 27; Clark 2022; Husserl’s “more richly articulated sense”; Langford’s formal analysis
(E)
The answer must explain why the F things are F. — Benson’s explanatoriness condition
1.
By (NC), any adequate answer to “What is F-ness?” introduces at least one concept G distinct from F-ness.
2.
By (E), the answer is adequate only if G does explanatory work — so A must know at least one thing about G, namely that it explains F.
3.
By (PD) applied to G, A cannot know anything about G without knowing what G-ness is.
4.
By (NC) applied to G, knowing what G-ness is introduces a further concept H distinct from G-ness.
∴
Knowing what F-ness is requires an unterminated chain of prior definitional knowledge. Either the chain terminates in concepts exempt from (PD), or nobody knows what anything is.

Stated as a dilemma, to match the family:

Horn 1 — exemptionSome concepts in the definiens are known without prior definition. Then (PD) is false as a general principle, and Socrates has given no reason why F-ness should not be among the exempt. The burden shifts to him.
Horn 2 — no exemptionEvery concept in the definiens falls under (PD). Then (PD) is unsatisfiable: the demand can never be met by anyone, and Socrates’ own practice of proposing and testing definitions is unintelligible.

This is the improvement. The old version had only the second horn, and “that’s an infinite regress” invites the reply “so stop somewhere.” The first horn makes stopping the problem rather than the solution: every place you stop is a concession that the principle has exceptions, and the principle contains nothing that says where they fall.

Clark walks into horn 1, in his own words. He needs stage one — the conceptual investigation — to be startable, so he grants that “it is plausible that competent speakers will have some initial grasp, at least, of the primary intension.” That is an exemption from (PD), granted to the definiens’s concepts, on the ground of linguistic competence. It is the same move as the cultural-inheritance defence, and the paper already has the answer to it in §4.4: a genuine cultural given cannot be denied by a competent member of the culture, and Gorgias denied one. Competence supplies familiarity, not warrant.

Objection II — the category

As it stood: “let say we say F is e1, F is e2… but then we are defining a category, let say e, that F belongs to. but then again without definition of F we can not do that.”

Reformulated: the circularity horn, run at the extension

This is the objection with the strongest position in the family, because it is the one that defeats Frege’s escape — the only escape in the six that actually dissolves the paradox rather than relocating it. Frege escapes by saying coincidence of extension is necessary and sufficient. The objection says: you cannot fix the extension without already having the concept. To collect e1, e2, e3 as the F things rather than as an arbitrary list is already to have exercised the very competence the definition was supposed to supply.

Horn 1 — the collection is principledThen a principle of collection is in hand, and that principle is the definition, or entails it — so the answer was presupposed by the question’s own materials.
Horn 2 — the collection is not principledThen it is a list, and coextension with a list is worthless: any accidentally coincident predicate satisfies it. The extension does not determine the definition.

Dummett makes horn 2 against Frege in exactly this form: the two definitions of a conic “are not merely coextensive: they are provably coextensive,” and a predicate merely materially equivalent to ‘conic’ “obviously would not be accepted by mathematicians as an admissible definition.” That is Socrates rejecting the god-beloved as a definition of the pious, in a mathematical example. Same objection, and it is already in print — against Frege, by Frege’s best reader, with no idea that it is Socratic.

Objection III — the relata

As it stood: “these questions assume what x is something separate different from idea about x… how would Socrates know F-ness without knowing x is F? or F-ness is G? if he can then knowing F-ness mean nothing more than x is F and F-ness is G.”

Reformulated: what individuates F-ness apart from the truths about it?

Lewy’s version of the paradox needs propositions individuated by the concepts they contain. This objection asks the corresponding question one level down, about the object: what individuates F-ness apart from the true propositions about it? (PD) asserts a priority — knowledge of the essence before knowledge of the instances and properties. The priority is intelligible only if the essence is something over and above the instances and properties.

Horn 1 — the essence is separateThen (PD) is intelligible but unmotivated: nothing in the question shows that such an item exists, and the paper’s (E) and (X) presuppositions are doing the work uncontested. This is where the Forms get posited — and where they cost what §6 says they cost.
Horn 2 — the essence is not separateThen knowing what F-ness is just is knowing that x is F and that F-ness is G — the instances and the properties. But those are exactly what (PD) says cannot be known first. The priority inverts.

Fine concedes the same ground when she declines to generalise from the Meno’s ousia of bees and flags the cost: if something lacks an essence, it cannot be known. Horn 2 is that cost collected.

§4  Where this leaves the objection relative to the literature

  • What is genuinely new The family is about statements — a single analysis and its dual role. The objection is about questions and their iteration. Nobody in the six is asking what licenses the asking. That is the erotetic turn, and it is the paper’s claim to a place.
  • The target moves In the family, horn 1 is a defect in the analysis — it is trivial, or circular. In the reformulation, horn 1 is a defect in the principle: (PD) must grant exemptions and contains nothing that says where. The conclusion is therefore not Husserl’s (analysis is impossible) but the weaker and more defensible one: the demand is unwarranted as stated.
  • The Socratic case is the hard case, not a derivative one Frege’s escape is available to the analytic tradition and unavailable to Socrates, who refuses coextension as sufficient at Euthyphro 11a–b. So the ancient application is not a footnote to the modern debate. It is the debate with the cheapest exit closed.
  • Where it is weaker, and what must be defended The paradox of analysis is self-contained: one statement, two roles, done. The regress needs the extra premise that (PD) governs the definiens’s concepts too. That premise is deniable — by Benson’s sufficiency condition, by Fine’s stepping-stone reading, and by Clark’s stage-ordering. The reformulation earns its keep by making every one of those denials a taking of horn 1, so the burden is theirs: name the exempt class and say what makes it exempt.
