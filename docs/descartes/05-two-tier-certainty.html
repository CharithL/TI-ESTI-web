The Two Tiers of First Certainty

“Thinking is occurring” and “this is a doubting”: does the cogito’s immunity pass from the first to the second, and has anyone said that it does not?

Chat“Research task” (two-tier)
Date17 July 2026 · DEscartes project
What the chat produced“The Two-Tier Structure of Cartesian First Certainty: A Literature Survey and Gap Analysis”
Checked hereDescartes, Broughton, Paul, Frankfurt, Kant (Guyer/Wood), Longuenesse

Abstract

This chat is different from the others. The brief states a thesis of yours and sends the literature to test it. Tier 1 is occurrence-certainty: “thinking is occurring now.” It is grounded in the self-defeat of any act that denies its own occurrence, it holds for any act of thinking, and it is demon-proof and thin (Lichtenberg’s “there is thinking”). Tier 2 is mode-and-framework certainty: “this act is a doubting, done under the method’s norms, and I have the concepts doubt, thought, certainty.” It is needed for the inventory of Meditation II, for Broughton’s dependence arguments and for the truth rule, yet it rests only on the transparency doctrine, which Wilson says Descartes never defends against the demon. Master question: does the literature distinguish the tiers, and does Tier 1’s immunity transfer to Tier 2 on any published account?

The chat’s answer is that the thesis has not been stated as such. It has three near neighbours: Paul 2018 (incorrigible “I am thinking” versus clear and distinct self-knowledge), Simmons 2012 (degrees of consciousness), and Broughton 2002 (“carefully worded self-reports”). The scholastic pair actus exercitus / actus signatus maps onto the two tiers and has never been applied to the cogito. The chat also corrects the brief in two places. Descartes does not concede that any action yields existence, only any cogitatio. And “tautological” is not Kant’s word at A355.

What this review adds. Broughton, whom the chat had not read in full, turns out to say explicitly that Descartes does not ground the certainty of self-reports in the transparency definition at AT 7:160. She grounds it in their relation to the possibility of doubt, and she adds that “Descartes is not claiming incorrigibility for these self-reports; he is claiming indubitability” (p. 137). That is the sharpest published statement of the gap your thesis names, and also the sharpest reply to it. Wilson’s Descartes, which the chat could only cite at second hand, is in your folder under a misleading filename, and her charge is confirmed word for word (p. 66). Two smaller findings: CSM translates AT 7:160 as “immediately aware,” so the chat’s note on translation variants is reversed. And Kant’s B422n, which the chat cites as anticipating the performative reading, reads Descartes as inferring existence: “as Descartes held.”

The dilemma the thesis grows from

Doubt is a deliberate act of will. For the method to work, the meditator seems to need to know which act he is performing. Either he cannot be certain of the act-type, or he can.

Horn A. If he cannot, the cogito’s certainty does not come from the method of doubt. It comes from the self-verification available to any conscious act, and the method is “idle scaffolding with respect to the famous certainty (it locates the survivor; it does not make it survive).”

Horn B. If he can, a certainty richer than bare thinking precedes or accompanies the cogito: certainty of a determinate mode (“this is a doubting”), plus the framework concepts. That rests on the transparency doctrine. And the doubt is performed in Meditation I but certified only in Meditation II (AT 7:28), after “I am, I exist” (AT 7:25). “The method runs before its own self-certification.”

How to read the citations. Descartes is cited by AT and CSM, checked. Kant is cited from the Guyer/Wood translation (Cambridge, 1998), which is in the folder’s kant subfolder as an unnamed file (31555070…pdf). Longuenesse’s I, Me, Mine is also there; its text layer carries no page numbers, so the chat’s p. 74 is reported, with the wording checked. Simmons (2012), Radner (1988), Mackie (1964) and Nuchelmans are not held. Wilson’s Descartes (1978) is held, although the chat treated it as unavailable. It is Descartes-Arguments-of-the-Philosophers-.pdf, which the folder’s filename audit misattributes to M. J. Inwood. Wilson is cited by that copy’s pagination.

The thing that makes this chat worth reading twice

Tier 2, not Tier 1, is the exposed flank. Every reading secures “thinking is occurring”: performative, intuitive, and conditions-of-doubt. But Descartes goes on to claim, “even if he who created me is doing all he can to deceive me,” that it is doubting and understanding and willing that is going on (T3). That richer claim rests on something the cogito’s own mechanism does not supply.

The chat’s systematic point follows from this. The truth rule is drawn from the cogito’s being a clear and distinct perception, a mode-level property (§10). “The generalization of the truth rule requires Tier 2. If Tier 2 is demon-vulnerable, the rule cannot launch.” That is the systematic-role problem of Chat 4, relocated one level down.

The thesis in two sentences
Tier 1 · actus exercitus
The act as exercised: something is being thought. Its denial defeats itself in any medium, because entertaining any content is already thinking. It is demon-proof and thin.
Tier 2 · actus signatus
The act as signified: this is a doubting, under these norms, with these concepts. It needs a concept under which the act is classified, and the demon has not been shown unable to corrupt that classification.
The scholastic labels are the chat’s proposal (§12). “Stating the two-tier thesis using this pair would be a novel contribution.”

01Doubt as an act of will, and the sequencing problem

T1 · Principles I.32

“…desire, aversion, assertion, denial and doubt are various modes of willing.”

AT 8A:17 · CSM 1:204 checked

If doubt is willed, it is an act of a determinate type, and the method needs its agent to be performing that type. That is the root of Horn B.

Meditation I: the doubt is performed
AT 7:17–23. The meditator doubts, supposes the demon, and resolves to treat the doubtful as false.
Meditation II, AT 7:25: “I am, I exist”
Certified first (CSM 2:17).
Meditation II, AT 7:28: “it is I who am doubting”
The self-attribution of the act that did the work is certified only now (T3). “The method runs before its own self-certification.”

The chat’s finding on this sequencing problem (Q5): “no commentator who flags it as a distinct problem in the terms the prompt uses.” The nearest are Gueroult, whose separation of the order of knowing from the order of being would defuse it, and Hintikka 1978, Williams, Carriero, Hatfield and Cottingham, who discuss the meditator’s progressive self-understanding without isolating the gap.

02The two tiers, defined

Tier 1 · occurrence-certainty: “thinking is occurring now”
Grounded in the logic of act-reflexive denial. Thinking is special only as the universal medium: entertaining any content is already thinking, unlike “I am not speaking,” which self-defeats only when spoken. Method-independent, demon-proof, and minimal in content.
secured on every account
Tier 2 · mode-and-framework certainty: “this act is a doubting, and I possess the concepts”
Required by the res cogitans inventory (AT 7:28), by Broughton-style dependence arguments, and by the eventual operation of the clarity rule. Grounded, on the thesis, only in the transparency axiom.
exposed

The brief distinguishes three doctrines that are “often run together”: infallibility (seeming → being), self-intimation (being → seeming), and mode-classification accuracy (this act is a doubting, not a hoping). The chat’s central negative finding is that “no account isolates mode-classification (c) as a third doctrine with its own demon-vulnerability.”

03The Gassendi concession, corrected

T2 · Fifth Replies

“When you say that I ‘could have made the same inference from any one of my other actions’ you are far from the truth, since I am not wholly certain of any of my actions, with the sole exception of thought (in using the word ‘certain’ I am referring to metaphysical certainty, which is the sole issue at this point). I may not, for example, make the inference ‘I am walking, therefore I exist’, except in so far as the awareness of walking is a thought.”

AT 7:352 · CSM 2:244 checked

The brief quoted the second sentence, “…the same applies to other actions,” as a concession that any conscious act suffices. The first sentence shows the opposite. Any mode of thinking suffices; no bodily action does. Horn A survives in corrected form: any cogitatio is enough, so doubt is not specially needed for Tier 1.

04Demon-proof modes, and the definition of thought

T3 · the inventory · Med. II

“Are not all these things just as true as the fact that I exist, even if I am asleep all the time, and even if he who created me is doing all he can to deceive me? … The fact that it is I who am doubting and understanding and willing is so evident that I see no way of making it any clearer.”

AT 7:28–29 · CSM 2:19 checked

C2 confirmed. The demon is invoked in the same breath as the modes, and waved away. This is the passage on which Tier 2 stands or falls.

T4 · the definition · Second Replies

“Thought. I use this term to include everything that is within us in such a way that we are immediately aware of it. Thus all the operations of the will, the intellect, the imagination and the senses are thoughts.”

AT 7:160 · CSM 2:113 checked — Latin ut eius immediate conscii simus

Correction. The chat’s quotation appendix gives CSM as “immediately conscious” and says Anscombe and Geach render conscii as “aware.” The CSM text in your folder reads “immediately aware.” If the translation variant matters to the cogitatio/conscientia distinction, it cannot be pinned on CSM.

05The framework concepts: pre-reflective knowledge

T5 · Sixth Replies

“…It is quite sufficient that we should know it by that internal awareness which always precedes reflective knowledge. This inner awareness of one’s thought and existence is so innate in all men that, although we may pretend that we do not have it if we are overwhelmed by preconceived opinions …, we cannot in fact fail to have it.”

AT 7:422 · CSM 2:285 checked

Descartes’ answer to the question of how the meditator knows what thought and existence are: pre-reflectively and innately. Wilson’s escalation, as the chat reports it, is that even granting innate meanings, “the theory of the framework remains corrigible.”

09Q7: Kant and the tiers

T6 · B131–2

“The I think must be able to accompany all my representations; for otherwise something would be represented in me that could not be thought at all…”

Kant, Critique of Pure Reason, trans. Guyer and Wood p. 246 checked

T7 · B422n

“The ‘I think’ is, as has already been said, an empirical proposition, and contains within itself the proposition ‘I exist.’ But I cannot say ‘Everything that thinks, exists’; for then the property of thinking would make all beings possessing it into necessary beings. Hence my existence also cannot be regarded as inferred from the proposition ‘I think,’ as Descartes held (for otherwise the major premise, ‘Everything that thinks, exists’ would have to precede it), but rather it is identical with it.”

Guyer and Wood p. 453 checked

C5 is confirmed on substance: existence is “identical with,” not inferred from, the “I think.” But notice “as Descartes held.” Kant reads Descartes as a syllogist, which is the reading Descartes rejects in the Second Replies (CSM 2:100). Kant anticipates the performative reading against Descartes, not as an interpretation of him.

A355 and “tautological.” The chat could not verify “tautological” as Kant’s word at A355, and neither can this review. In the Guyer/Wood text, forms of “tautolog-” occur elsewhere but not in the A-Paralogism. The chat’s diagnosis stands: “a commentators’ gloss,” with the B-edition language conflated into A.

Kant criticizes Descartes and his rationalist followers for having been under the illusion that they could derive not only ‘I exist,’ but also an answer to the question ‘What am I?’ from the mere consideration of the proposition ‘I think.’ Longuenesse, I, Me, Mine (2017), ch. 4 — wording checked; p. 74 as the chat gives it (not legible in the text layer)

Kant, on Longuenesse’s reading, is the one major figure who holds the two-tier structure outright: “I exist” is secured, and “What am I?” is refused. But his line runs between existence and nature, not between occurrence and mode, so he maps the thesis only in part.

11Three near neighbours, and where each diverges

JB
Janet Broughton
UC Berkeley
Reads the Meditations through “dependence arguments”: certain propositions are shown indubitable because their truth is a condition of the possibility of methodic doubt.
Key work here
  • Descartes’s Method of Doubt (Princeton, 2002), ch. 6 “Using Doubt” (p. 100); ch. 7 “Inner Conditions” (pp. 105, 121, 133–7)
Where she matters: the sharpest statement of the gap, and the sharpest reply to it
1 · Paul 2018
the distinction, without the demon
Incorrigible “I am thinking” (p. 1088) versus clear and distinct self-knowledge (p. 1083). Diverges: the contrast is consciousness versus introspection, not occurrence versus mode. Radical doubt is the remedy that secures self-knowledge; on the thesis, Tier 2 stays exposed after doubt.
2 · Simmons 2012
degrees, without a certainty-gap
Types and degrees of consciousness allow being conscious that one thinks without clearly perceiving what. Diverges: a project in cognitive psychology, unconnected to the demon or the cogito’s foundational role. not held
3 · Broughton 2002
the demon, without the transparency
“(Of course, it will matter what exactly is to count as ‘doubting’ here.)” (p. 100). Diverges: careful wording is achievable indubitability, not a second tier with its own vulnerability. See below for why this matters more than the chat saw.
T8 · Broughton, ch. 7

“I think that here Descartes is trying to show that carefully worded self-reports about sensing are conditions of the possibility of engaging in methodic doubt.” … “But I don’t think that in the Second Meditation he means for this doctrine [AT 7:160] to serve as his ground for certainty about his carefully worded self-reports. He thinks instead that this certainty arises from the relation between the self-reports and the possibility of methodic doubt. … Descartes is not claiming incorrigibility for these self-reports; he is claiming indubitability.”

Broughton pp. 134, 137 checked

The thesis says Tier 2 rests only on the transparency axiom of AT 7:160. Broughton says explicitly that it does not: it rests on dependence on the possibility of doubt, and what it claims is indubitability, not incorrigibility. So she has isolated the question the thesis asks, which ground supports the mode claims, and answered it the other way. She is not a near neighbour but the principal opponent, and the thesis must show that her dependence arguments presuppose correct mode-classification. Her own p. 100 parenthesis is the opening.

12The unclaimed tool: actus exercitus / actus signatus

The chat’s recommendation
“State the thesis using the scholastic pair.”

The act as exercised (Tier 1: self-verifying by performance) against the act as signified (Tier 2: requiring a concept under which the act is classified). The distinction is documented in medieval semantics (Nuchelmans; Roger Bacon, Radulphus Brito, Kilwardby) and used by Gadamer in discussions of self-refutation. “I found no scholar who applies the pair to the cogito.” not held The chat itself marks this negative finding as provisional, “the medieval-semantics and Gadamer literatures are large.” The pair would also connect the thesis to the scholastic background of Chat 9.

13Internal audit and corrections

Corrections and additions

  1. Horn A, corrected by the chat and confirmed: AT 7:352 extends certainty to any cogitatio, “with the sole exception of thought” (CSM 2:244).
  2. AT 7:160 in CSM reads “immediately aware,” not “immediately conscious” (CSM 2:113). The chat’s variant note has the two translations the wrong way round, at least as far as CSM goes.
  3. Kant B422n says “as Descartes held.” Kant attributes the inferential cogito to Descartes and corrects it. C5 should read: Kant proposes the identity view against Descartes.
  4. Broughton pp. 134 and 137 are confirmed and say more than the chat reports: the certainty of self-reports is expressly not grounded in AT 7:160, and it is indubitability, not incorrigibility. C4 and the gap analysis should be revised accordingly.

14Where this leads

The line to keep

The cogito secures that thinking is going on. It does not by itself secure what kind of thinking it is, and the method of doubt, the inventory and the truth rule all need the second.

Chat 2 showed Derrida’s leverage falling on exactly this seam (§08). Chat 6 turns to the step from “a thought” to “a thinking thing”: Dicker’s substance theory, and your objection that we only ever know individual thoughts.
