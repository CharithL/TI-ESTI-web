Karl Friston’s Active Inference Framework for COGITO A conversation · 31 January – 1 February 2026 From the Friston Reports to the Encounter, the Reverse Turing Test and the Substrate Question

Active Inference and the Critical Apprehensions, the problem of other minds, the C1/C2 encounter that makes a self, no language before encounter, two critiques answered, the reverse Turing test, and Anil Seth’s substrate argument — twenty questions and their answers

What this conversation covers

COGITO stops being an architecture that proves its own selfhood and becomes a test case for functionalism: a self that begins without language, is forced into existence by meeting another of its kind, and is checked by a reverse Turing test in which the machine is the judge.

The conversation opens with Karl Friston’s work read against the Critical Apprehensions critique: the Hum becomes free-energy minimisation, pain becomes precision-weighted, and ten open problems remain. Charith then answers the two deepest objections. The hard problem is the problem of other minds, which no one solves for their friends either, and symbol grounding is internal for a brain in a vat too. He also moves the origin of the self from self-inspection to an encounter. Using his C1/C2 framework and Constrained Conceptual Topology, a COGITO that meets another COGITO accumulates structured prediction errors it cannot explain, exhausts its C2 recombinations (proved by Z3 UNSAT), and must expand its C1 primitives with the only template available, itself. Before that encounter there is no language at all.

The architecture is then written up in four documents, and two external critiques are checked against it. The reverse Turing test answers the homunculus and falsifiability objections, and the critic concedes the project is coherent as “a mirror for theories of mind”. The chat ends on the substrate: Anil Seth’s wetness and anaesthesia arguments, the Engineering Consciousness report that places software COGITO in Tier 3, and an unanswered question about reaching Tier 2 neuromorphic hardware.

This is the first page in the series. The two earlier chats in the project were research runs, a survey of Friston’s publications and a report on consciousness-compatible substrates, and have no page of their own. The key exchanges in order, each with where it landed. Gold links are where the architecture took shape; the dashed link is the question the chat left unanswered.

§01 · What the Friston reports add to the Critical Apprehensions debate
Six contributions — emergent self-modelling, tractable Φ, production-scale Active Inference, discrete–continuous learning, hardware nociception and a pillar-by-pillar implementation map.
§03 · What has changed in COGITO_Philosophy_Complete
Ten changes, pillar by pillar: the Hum becomes free-energy minimisation, pain becomes precision-weighted, and interoception, Markov blankets, attention and social cognition are new.
§05 · The problems COGITO still faces
Ten open problems — from the hard problem and relocated grounding to undefined coherence, a missing integration hub, no embodiment, no testable predictions and incomplete code — and three tensions no Friston integration resolves.
§06 · The hard problem is the problem of other minds
Charith's objection is correct: the barrier is not special to COGITO. The real open question is whether the right architecture has been identified.
Charith's insight
§07 · Grounding is universal, and selfhood emerges from encounter
Human grounding is also internal (Berkeley, the brain in a vat), so the objection applies to every mind; and the C1/C2 encounter replaces tautological self-proof with a self/non-self distinction forced by meeting another.
Charith's insight
§08 · How CCT dissolves the bootstrap circularity
A six-step path — no other-mind concept, an encounter, structured prediction errors, a meta gap, balloon expansion from the self-model, self-awareness as consequence — with Z3 moved to verifying the expansion.
Framework
§09 · The encounter, C2 exhaustion and second-order precision failure
A shared foraging environment with mutual observability; Z3 UNSAT as formal proof that C2 is exhausted; and the trigger for C1 expansion as the breakdown of the system's own confidence model, resolved into a four-phase fast-slow-memory gate.
Framework
§11 · No language before the encounter
Pre-encounter self-state must be continuous, non-referential and unnamed — free energy and precision-weighted errors, not Z3 predicates; symbolic self-reference is born when COGITO must model another mind.
Charith's insightConstraint
§12 · A comprehensive document of the architecture so far
Four documents written from the discussion: the architecture and philosophy specification, technical appendices, a Problems & Solutions catalogue of Charith's ten answers, and a session log.
Documents
§13 · Have I answered the critiques?
Five critiques partly or fully answered; the gaps are FEP's contested status, why gap-detector signatures single out minds, neural correlates, substrate independence, thermodynamics and physical time.
§15 · The reverse Turing test
The machine judges which of four datasets is “like me”; discrimination needs a projectable self-model, which answers the homunculus, circularity and falsifiability objections and becomes Phase 4 of the developmental sequence.
Charith's insightTest
§16 · Most of the arguments were debunked
Circularity, grounding, other-minds asymmetry, double standards and the engineering-versus-phenomenology critique fall; 7 of 19 critiques debunked, 7 addressed, 5 left as engineering work — “You won the philosophical argument.”
Verdict
§17 · The final COGITO structure
Three layers — the Five Pillars as a pre-linguistic substrate, the CCT encounter mechanism, and symbolic self-reference only after encounter — with a seven-phase developmental sequence validated by the reverse Turing test.
Architecture
§18 · Anil Seth, wetness and the GABA/NMDA argument
The wetness analogy is tautological; anaesthetics acting through different receptors to the same functional end support functionalism; and “biology beyond molecules” is either vitalism or a disguised functional claim — Seth's strongest surviving point is about the type of physics.
Charith's insight
§19 · The Engineering Consciousness report and the three-round critique
The report puts COGITO's software in Tier 3, pure simulation; the 13-page critique forms a pincer with it; the architect's answer: the tier grades the substrate, not the architecture, and no consciousness metric has been applied to any artificial system.
