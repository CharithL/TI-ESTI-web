The Immunity of the Cogito

Abstract

The brief asked one question under strict scope discipline: by what argument do commentators hold that the demon cannot falsify “I am thinking,” and by what further step does that yield “I exist”? Everything outside Meditation II was ruled out, including dualism, the Circle and the wax, on the model of Dicker’s refusal to import dualism before it is established. The chat returns a family of incompatible answers that share one vocabulary. They are performance (Ayer, Hintikka), intuition (Descartes, Gaukroger, Sarkar, Markie), self-reference (Gueroult), self-entailing doubt (Curley), substance-theoretic inference (Dicker), and diagnostic denial (Carriero).

The central result is where the fault line runs. It is not “inference or intuition?”; that debate is nearly exhausted. It is whether the immunity that attaches to “I am thinking” survives the transition to “I exist,” and how much the conclusion is entitled to assert. Arguments that secure the bare occurrence of thinking are routinely stretched to secure an identified subject and then a substance. Lichtenberg, Kenny and the later Hintikka locate the leak at exactly the point where the “I” is injected.

The chat’s principal negative finding: no commentator answers the memory dilemma while staying inside Meditation II. Every route to certainty across time runs through the later proof of God. This review adds one piece of evidence the chat did not have. Gueroult, the chat’s champion of the cogito’s unique privilege, says the same himself. The “certainty of my certainty” is “abolished in the recollection of my certainty,” and the cogito “then descends to the level of mathematical truths” (Gueroult I, p. 105). That concession largely dissolves the Gueroult–Carriero dispute the chat calls the sharpest in the field: they are describing the cogito at two different moments.

The question this chat asks, and the one it refuses

“The point is not to summarise the cogito literature. It is to isolate the immunity-mechanism each commentator relies on, state it in that commentator’s own words, and compare mechanisms across the literature.”

Asked: five sub-questions. What is protected (Q1)? By what mechanism (Q2)? How is the transition to “I exist” made, and does the immunity survive it (Q3)? What does the conclusion assert (Q4)? Is the immunity unique (Q5)?

Refused: anything that reaches past Meditation II. An account that needs the mind to be an immaterial substance, or needs God to secure present-tense certainty, is marked as having left the passage. The one exception is the boundary case of retention over time. That case is the memory dilemma, and it turns out to be where the survey ends.

The thing that makes this chat worth reading twice

The immunity question has three parts, and the literature answers the first and walks past the other two. Is “I am thinking” demon-proof? Almost everyone says yes, by one of six routes. Is the step to “I exist” demon-proof? This “is the crux and is routinely skipped.” And what does “I exist” assert: an event, a bearer, an identified subject, a substance? “This is where accounts most often over-deliver.”

The chat’s sharpest single observation is about Hintikka’s own retreat. On the performance reading there is no gap between “think” and “exist” for the demon to exploit. “The leak reappears not between ‘think’ and ‘exist’ but between ‘exist’ and ‘I exist.’”

The whole dispute in two sentences — reconstructed, not quoted
The privilege thesis
While the demon is in force, the cogito alone survives: arithmetic can be corrupted, “I am thinking” cannot. Its certainty is of a different kind.
The parity thesis
The cogito is certain because it is clearly and distinctly perceived, like any geometrical truth. Its first place is a matter of order, not of a special logical property.
Gueroult holds the first (p. 27) and Carriero the second (p. 78). The chat treats them as rivals. Gueroult holds both, at different moments: the privilege while the cogito is actually intuited, parity once it is only remembered (p. 105). See §11.

The scope rule that governs the whole survey

“Hold every author to Dicker’s discipline and report who violates it.” The cogito’s structure must be worked out without importing dualism, because at AT 7:25 dualism has not been established and using it would be circular. Two backward transpositions are flagged throughout. One is Meditation III’s claim that ideas “considered solely in themselves … cannot strictly speaking be false” (AT 7:37, CSM 2:26 checked). The other is the divine guarantee. Dicker’s own reconstruction is the subject of Chat 6.

Prologue

About this part

The text the whole survey is confined to, and the five things a commentator might claim it protects.

In short

Descartes’ own sentences already separate the items: “I am thinking” is necessarily true while conceived, lasts only while I think, and names a thing whose nature is not yet known.

The passage and the ladder.

“Commentators are not defending the same claim.” Before any mechanism is compared, the survey fixes what is being protected, because most over-delivery happens by sliding up a ladder of claims without noticing.

01The passage, in six sentences

T1 · the demon conceded, and defeated · Med. II

“…let him deceive me as much as he can, he will never bring it about that I am nothing so long as I think that I am something.”

AT 7:25 · CSM 2:17 checked

T2 · the certainty · Med. II

“…I must finally conclude that this proposition, I am, I exist, is necessarily true whenever it is put forward by me or conceived in my mind.”

AT 7:25 · CSM 2:17 checked · quoties a me profertur, vel mente concipitur

T3 · its duration · Med. II

“I am, I exist — that is certain. But for how long? For as long as I am thinking.”

AT 7:27 · CSM 2:18 checked · quandiu autem? nempe quandiu cogito

T4 · its yield · Med. II

“I am, then, in the strict sense only a thing that thinks; that is, I am a mind, or intelligence, or intellect, or reason — words whose meaning I have been ignorant of until now.”

AT 7:27 · CSM 2:18 checked · sum igitur praecise tantum res cogitans

“Words whose meaning I have been ignorant of until now”: Descartes is naming a subject he does not yet understand, not deriving a substance. The chat reads praecise tantum as a restriction on the order of knowledge. That reading is what keeps T4 inside Meditation II.

T5 · not a syllogism · Second Replies

“…he does not deduce existence from thought by means of a syllogism, but recognizes it as something self-evident by a simple intuition of the mind.”

AT 7:140 · CSM 2:100 checked

T6 · and yet a prior notion · Principles I.10

“…because these are very simple notions, and ones which on their own provide us with no knowledge of anything that exists, I did not think they needed to be listed.”

AT 8A:8 · CSM 1:196 checked

“In order to think it is necessary to exist” is required, and yet on its own yields no knowledge of existence. Markie builds his hybrid reading on the tension between T5 and T6 (§06).

02What is protected: five candidate items

(a) The bare occurrence of thinking
Granted demon-proof by nearly everyone.
secure
(b) My knowledge that thinking is occurring
The self-intimation of (a). Also granted.
secure
(c) My knowledge that I am the one thinking
“Ownership (c) is where the trouble starts.” This is the step Lichtenberg denies and the later Hintikka concedes.
contested
(d) My knowledge of what I am thinking
Conceded not to be demon-proof at this stage. It can be rescued only by importing Med. III’s ideas-cannot-be-false, which is a backward transposition.
not at AT 7:25
(e) That thinking is a mode of me
The ontological claim behind res cogitans. Reached, if at all, by a different and weaker argument.
weaker argument

The chat’s roster: (a) and (b) are held demon-proof by Ayer, Hintikka 1962, Williams 1962, Gueroult, Curley, Gaukroger, Sarkar, Markie, Dicker, Stroud and Newman. (c) is claimed only by Williams 1962 (“the infallible power of ‘I’ to refer”) and by Gueroult’s identity. It is denied or doubted by Lichtenberg, Kenny, Hintikka 1996, Sorell and Williams 1978. “Several arguments that succeed for (a)/(b) are silently extended to (c)”: the chat calls this the single most common defect in the literature.

Part One

About this part

Each mechanism stated in its author’s own words, with the locus of the immunity named.

In short

There are three loci: the act, the content and the hypothesis. They are not notational variants; they can disagree in verdict. Intuition denies there is any joint. Carriero denies there is anything special. Dicker supplies a bearer.

Sections 03–08performance · self-reference · self-entailing doubt · intuition · denial · distinctness
01

Six mechanisms, three loci.

“Many authors reach the same conclusion by incompatible routes; several use the same vocabulary for different arguments. The deliverable’s value lies in separating them.”

The chat’s analytic contribution is the question where each account puts the immunity. “These three are conflated constantly in the secondary literature, and untangling them is the main analytic yield.”

03In the act: performance

The chat credits A. J. Ayer, The Problem of Knowledge (1956), ch. 2, with the earliest clean statement. The demon “cannot deceive me into believing that I am thinking when I am not, for if I believe that I am thinking, then I must believe truly, since my believing that I am thinking is itself a process of thought.” Hence “there was therefore no need for Descartes to derive ‘sum’ from ‘cogito’.” not held The folder’s Ayer-on-knowing.pdf is Peter Naur’s 1993 critique of Ayer’s book, not the book itself, so these sentences could not be checked.

Hintikka’s 1962 statement, which the literature treats as the origin, is held:

…the inconsistency (absurdity) of an existentially inconsistent statement can in a sense be said to be of performatory (performative) character. It depends on an act or “performance,” namely on a certain person’s (here, a’s) uttering the sentence… Hintikka, “Cogito, Ergo Sum: Inference or Performance?” Phil. Review 71 (1962) p. 12 checked
The priority claim
“Ayer has genuine priority over Hintikka for the criterion, and the standard histories under-credit him.”

Ayer’s book is six years earlier and, on the chat’s reading, states the self-verification criterion with the same structure. Williams (1962) credits Ayer explicitly. Hintikka’s performance reading is given a page of its own in Chat 4, which follows its five stages to the 1996/2000 retreat.

04In the content: Gueroult’s self-reference

MG
Martial Gueroult
1891–1976 · Strasbourg; Sorbonne; Collège de France
Historian of philosophy whose Descartes selon l’ordre des raisons (1953) reads the Meditations as a chain of reasons whose meaning depends on position in the order.
Key work here
  • Descartes’ Philosophy Interpreted According to the Order of Reasons, vol. I, trans. Ariew (1984): ch. III §1, p. 27; ch. V, p. 105
Where he matters: §04 (Type 5), and §§11–12, where his own ch. V reframes the chat’s two live disputes. His quarrel with Alquié is Chat 8.

For Gueroult, universal doubt is the rule and the cogito is an exception carved within it. The chat quotes the passage accurately; here it is with its page:

This first certainty, which is confined within itself, in no way destroys universal doubt outside of itself; it introduces an exception to it. It introduces an exception of fact, since universal doubt, which is based on the evil genius, remains as rule; yet it is also necessary, since we are constrained to conceive that what we are verifying in this case cannot be otherwise. Gueroult I, ch. III §1 p. 27 checked
Universal doubt would apply only to what the I affirmed as an existential or essential truth valid outside of itself. Here the I affirms only itself: the object posited is nothing but the subject. … I acquire a certainty of my certainty that is infinitely superior to the initial certainty of [mathematics]. Gueroult I p. 27 checked

Immunity locus: the content’s self-reference. There is nothing outside the proposition for doubt to grip. The chat marks Gueroult as borderline on scope: he goes straight on to describe the thinking being as an “abstract being” conceived as a totum apart from everything else, one step from importing the real distinction.

05In the hypothesis: Curley’s self-entailing doubt

Any supposition that explains how my thinking might be erroneous must entail not only that I exist, but also that I think. So “I think” would also survive systematic doubt, and the two propositions are on a par. Curley, Descartes Against the Skeptics p. 88 checked
Still, it may be argued, to make this requirement is to stack the cards in Descartes’ favor. A skeptic who is sufficiently on his guard to see what Descartes is up to will reject the requirement. Why should he offer any explanation of my error? Curley p. 88 checked

Immunity locus: the skeptical hypothesis itself. The demon’s story cannot be told without conceding the point. The chat adds Curley’s caution that the object whose immunity is at issue is unstable in Descartes’ own text. Descartes “consistently blurs the distinction between inferences and propositions by referring to the whole formula ‘I think, therefore I am’ as a truth, a first principle, a proposition, and a conclusion” p. 79 checked.

06No joint at all: intuition, thin and radical

Descartes’ own description (T5) makes the cogito a simplex mentis intuitus: there is no inferential joint for the demon to attack. The chat’s correction to the taxonomy is that Type 2 comes in two strengths.

Thin intuition
1
Descartes, Second Replies
No syllogism; he “learns it from experiencing in his own case that it is impossible that he should think without existing” (CSM 2:100).
hybrid

“…Descartes intuits the self-evident proposition that he thinks and simultaneously immediately infers that he exists” (p. 145 checked). He names it “the self-evident intuition/immediate inference interpretation” (p. 148).
Radical intuition

In the limiting case “inference comes down to the intuitive grasp of a necessary connection between premiss and conclusion” (p. 53 checked). Rules of inference are not needed, so a logic-corrupting demon gains nothing.

Only the radical version earns the uniqueness verdict of §11. The chat’s further correction is that no major commentator defends a purely syllogistic cogito. Type 1 survives only as the immediate-inference hybrid, and the chat recommends renaming it to avoid the strawman.

Type 4, Dicker. Thought is a property; properties need a bearer; so a thinker exists. The bearer is a bare logical subject, not yet an immaterial substance. The chat treats Dicker as “the model of scope discipline.” His six-step reconstruction, and whether its first premise can be had without dualism, is the whole of Chat 6.

Type 7, Almog. The chat reports Almog’s Cogito? Descartes and Thinking the World as “located but not obtained” and declines to build on it. The book is in your folder (Cogito-Descartes-and-Thinking-the-World.pdf, Oxford 2008), and its preface states the frame directly. For Descartes, “thinking—penser—is essentially penser-le-monde, so well put in French, without a mediative preposition such as ‘about’ or ‘of’” p. vii checked. Type 7 can therefore be sourced from the book itself rather than from secondary report.

08Are Types 3, 5 and 5b one mechanism or three?

3 · the act
Ayer · Hintikka
The doing verifies the sentence. It needs no theory of what doubt ranges over. A being who never reflected on doubt could run it.
5 · the content
Gueroult
A claim about the logical geography of doubt: the proposition posits only its own subject. It needs no theory of speech acts.
5b · the hypothesis
Curley
A tu quoque against the skeptic. It is indifferent to whether the cogito is act or proposition, and it works even for a silently entertained hypothesis, with no performance at all.

The chat’s verdict is that they “are not notational variants”: they come apart under pressure and can disagree in verdict. It adds a new row, Type 3′, “doubt-dispelling.” This is Broughton’s reading and the Frankfurt–Curley line, on which the cogito’s job is to show “I am” indubitable rather than true, because the grounds for doubt presuppose it. Its locus is the structure of the doubting project itself.

Part Two

About this part

Q3 and Q4: the step from “I am thinking” to “I exist,” and how much “I exist” says.

In short

Almost no one argues that the demon can grant “I am thinking” and block “I exist.” The real gap is further up: from “something exists” to “I exist,” and from there to a substance.

10The yield: event, bearer, subject, substance

The chat’s ladder for Q4: “I exist” might assert an event, a bearer, an identified subject or a substance. The critics of the yield each block the ladder at a rung.

T7 · Lichtenberg, Sudelbuch K 76 · the German

“Wir kennen nur allein die Existenz unserer Empfindungen, Vorstellungen und Gedanken. Es denkt, sollte man sagen, so wie man sagt: es blitzt. Zu sagen cogito, ist schon zu viel, so bald man es durch Ich denke übersetzt. Das Ich anzunehmen, zu postulieren, ist praktisches Bedürfnis.”

The later Hintikka. The chat quotes “Cogito Ergo Quis Est?” from Revue Internationale de Philosophie 1/1996. That text is in the folder as hintikka 1996.pdf, and the French version of 2000 is there too. All three of the chat’s claims can be read on the page:

Indeed, the weak point of my original reading of the cogito was the question of what happens to the presuppositions of a performatory demonstration in the first-person case. … I assumed quietly in my original paper that everyone knows who one is oneself. For all these reasons, the Cartesian cogito scarcely cannot support any inference to the existence of a public object, let alone a substance. In so far as the res in Descartes’ formula sum res cogitans is supposed to be a res publica, this alleged conclusion just does not follow from the cogito. Hintikka 1996 p. 14 checked — “scarcely cannot” is the printed text, an evident slip for “scarcely can,” which is how the chat quotes it; RMM 2000 p. 22

Williams and Sorell. Williams 1978 concludes that the Lichtenberg-style objector “shares a basic error with Descartes. The chat’s verdict on res cogitans
“I exist” is demon-proof. “I am a thinking thing” is demon-proof only as the epistemic-order claim of praecise tantum. “I am an immaterial substance” is not established until Meditation VI.

The trajectory of agreement runs from Gassendi (even granting the thinking subject, it need not be immaterial) through Lichtenberg and Kenny to Hintikka 1996. Any account that reads AT 7:27 as already delivering an immaterial substance violates Dicker’s discipline.

Part Three

About this part

The two disputes the chat reports as open, and a third question about Williams.

In short

Gueroult’s chapter V turns both disputes. The privilege of the cogito is real but momentary; memory abolishes it; only God restores it. The chat’s negative finding is right, and Gueroult is its best witness.

Sections 11–13Gueroult/Carriero · memory · Williams
03

The live disputes.

“A well-documented open question is worth more here than a manufactured consensus.” The brief’s standard, applied to the chat’s own adjudications.

11Gueroult against Carriero, and Gueroult against himself

The chat’s adjudication is careful. The dispute is “partly verbal and partly substantive.” Gueroult asks what survives while the demon is in force; Carriero asks what kind of epistemic state the cogito is. On the narrow question of doubt-resistance under an active demon, “Gueroult (and Sarkar) have the better of the text,” because AT 7:21 lets the demon corrupt arithmetic and AT 7:25 does not let it corrupt the cogito. “Carriero’s ‘same footing’ is defensible only at the later stage.” And “I find no published piece that stages the Gueroult/Carriero disagreement head-on.”

Gueroult stages it himself, in chapter V, three chapters after the passage the chat quotes:

T8 · the privilege abolished · Gueroult I, ch. V

“My certainty, properly speaking, which was upheld by my actual intuition, therefore disappears in order to be replaced by the memory of my certainty and my intuition. … I am immediately seized by the doubt raised by the hypothesis of the deceiving God. Thus the certainty of my certainty, the privilege of the Cogito, is abolished in the recollection of my certainty. The Cogito then descends to the level of mathematical truths, meaning to the level of these ideas that are certain while one contemplates them, but that, in spite of their clearness and distinctness, do not confirm by themselves the certainty of their certainty…”

Gueroult I, ch. V (“The First Proof of God’s Existence”) p. 105 checked

This is Carriero’s “same footing,” asserted by Gueroult, for the cogito as remembered. So the two are not rivals over one object. The intuited cogito outranks mathematics (Gueroult p. 27). The recollected cogito is on a par with it (Gueroult p. 105; Carriero p. 78).

What this changes in the chat’s adjudication

The chat’s verdict, Gueroult for the active demon and Carriero for the later stage, is right. But it is Gueroult’s own verdict, not a compromise imposed between two rivals. The disagreement that remains is narrower: whether Carriero’s parity also holds during the intuition. That is the question of whether a clear and distinct geometrical perception, while attended to, resists the active demon as the cogito does. Carriero says yes (“while I am having a clear perception, I am getting to truth,” p. 78); Gueroult says no.

12The memory dilemma

But there is a dilemma facing Descartes that I am unable to resolve. Either memory plays a role in the cogito-state of a thinker, or it does not. If memory plays a role, then of course the certainty of the cogito is spurious, because memory, at the present stage in the investigation, is not yet known to be reliable… Sarkar, Descartes’ Cogito: Saved from the Great Shipwreck p. 194 checked

The other horn, as the chat states it: if memory plays no role, the meditator cannot retain the skeptical arguments that motivated the doubt. Huet anticipated the core in 1689, holding that thinking that I am thinking needs two thoughts, “the later, current one on the previous, earlier one” (Lennon trans., c. pp. 82–83) not held. The Huet dossier is in Chat 1 §06.

The chat’s principal negative finding, confirmed
No commentator secures the cogito’s certainty across time while staying inside Meditation II.

Gueroult’s chapter V is the clearest statement of why, and it is by the author the chat treats as the cogito’s strongest defender. Whatever answers the dilemma has to be of the kind Derrida says it must be in Chat 2: something that holds the instant together from outside it. Descartes’ name for that is continuous creation, and his guarantor is God.
