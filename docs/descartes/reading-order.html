Abstract

Each chat in the project gets one page here. Every page is built on the structure of your Socratic review page: an abstract, the question, a works table, part openers, text panels with page-numbered quotations, an internal audit, and an interactive map. Each page does three things.

  • It reports what the chat argued.
  • It checks every quotation it can against the books in your folder. Each quotation is marked checked when found on the page given, and not held when the source is not in the folder.
  • It adds what the folder shows that the chat could not see.

The order below is not the order the chats were held. Read chronologically, they start in the middle (Hintikka, 15 July) and circle back to the doubt a week later. Read in the order given here, they make one argument. First, the doubt and its strongest objection. Then the answer, step by step, from “the demon cannot touch this” to “so I am a thinking thing.” Then a turn back on the whole enterprise: by what right we reconstruct it, what the untranslated French half of the debate says, and where the inference came from.

What the reviews found, in one line. The chats are reliable on argument and less reliable on locators. Across the nine pages, the most frequent problems are these: a quotation assigned to the wrong objector or the wrong Replies; a paraphrase presented as a quotation; a translation variant; and a source reported as unobtainable that was sitting in your folder. Section 03 collects them.

The question the nine chats share

What does “I am, I exist” actually give us, and by what right do we say so?

Two halves. Chats 1–6 work on the first half: what the doubt can reach, what escapes it, and what the escape yields: an act, an occurrence, a self, a substance. Chats 7–9 work on the second: whose cogito this is, the text’s, the reconstructor’s or Augustine’s, and whether the method of reading has already decided the answer.

How to read the pages. Descartes is cited by AT volume and page and by CSM (vols. 1–2) or CSMK (vol. 3) page, e.g. “AT 7:25 · CSM 2:17.” p. 42 tags give the printed page of the work named. checked means the words were found on that page in your copy. not held means the source is not in the folder, so the chat’s wording is reported as the chat gave it. [E] marks what a scholar said; [P] marks the chat’s or this review’s own assessment. Translations from French are this review’s unless stated.

The finding that crosses the most pages

One inference carries the argument from Chat 5 to Chat 9: from a thought to a thing that thinks. Dicker formalises it (Chat 6: CSM 2:124, 2:156, with nihili nulla sunt attributa behind them). At Royaumont, Alquié grounds his “being beyond concept” on the same texts, and Gueroult calls the result an occult quality (Chat 8). Descartes tells Colvius that this use, not the inference itself, is what separates him from Augustine (Chat 9). No chat saw this, because no chat read the others. It appears only when the pages are read in this order.

03What the reviews found

The single most consequential correction or addition on each page. Every page has its full list in its internal-audit section.

By page

  1. Hyperbolic doubt. “Adopting a new prejudice” is Gassendi’s objection (Fifth Objections, CSM 2:180), not Bourdin’s. The Bouwsma “quotations” are paraphrase; his words are at pp. 145 and 150. Peirce’s “uneasy and dissatisfied state” is from 1877 (EP1 114), not 1868.
  2. Derrida. The six-step chain is the chat’s teaching device, not Derrida’s own argument. Gombay and the chat also describe the 1963 reply to Foucault differently, so cite the essay itself.
  3. Immunity. Hintikka’s 1996 “Cogito, ergo quis est?” is held in English and French. Ayer-on-knowing.pdf is not Ayer: it is Peter Naur’s critique of The Problem of Knowledge.
  4. Hintikka. The 1962 paper already offers two interpretations (pp. 25, 31), which weakens the charge that Hintikka made performance the only reading. Non-generalisability is at p. 21.
  5. Two tiers. CSM 2:113 reads “immediately aware,” not “conscious.” Broughton’s certainty is indubitability, not incorrigibility (pp. 134, 137). Kant’s B422n says “as Descartes held.”
  6. Dicker. The reply to Hobbes is CSM 2:124, not 123. Dicker’s own step 13 (p. 79) is where your objection bites.
  7. Method. The three items the chat could not verify are all in your folder: Hintikka’s confession (RIP 1996 p. 15), the Royaumont exchange (p. 42), and Williams’s chapter. Gueroult himself calls the Meditations “the initiation of one soul by another soul” (p. 9).
  8. Alquié. Bannan, Beck and Ariew, “located but not obtained,” are all held, and none says what the chat expected. The Henry–Alquié lineage is stated in English by Marion (p. 197 n. 21). Paul’s anti-transparency is a different thesis from Alquié’s.
  9. Augustine. The reading protocol is structurally correct throughout. It misses Menn’s own erratum (Bérulle, 1627 not 1628, p. xiv), and the notes it was written for have not yet been produced.

04Notes on your library

Two “safe to rename” entries in FILENAME_AUDIT.md are wrong

Section A of the audit lists authors “identified — safe to rename.” Two of them are the series, not the author:

  • Descartes-Arguments-of-the-Philosophers-.pdf is listed under “Hegel M.J.Inwood.” Its title page reads “DESCARTES · Margaret Dauler Wilson” (Routledge, 1978). Inwood wrote the Hegel volume in the same series. This is the Wilson that Chats 4 and 5 cite.
  • Descartes-among-the-Scholastics.pdf (and the highlited copy) is listed under “M. Feingold.” Feingold is the series editor. The book is “By Roger Ariew” (Brill, 2011).

Renaming as the audit suggests would mislabel two of the most-cited books in the project.
